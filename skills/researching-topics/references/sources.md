# Researching topics source map

Use this file to audit a lesson, not as additional workflow. The package synthesizes information-
literacy standards, empirical source-evaluation research, evidence-review methods, citation guidance,
and practitioner heuristics. It does not copy any one research manual.

Verified 7 August 2026 against the linked pages.

## Scoping and iterative search

- [ACRL Framework for Information Literacy for Higher Education](https://www.ala.org/acrl/standards/ilframework)
  — basis for treating research as iterative inquiry, choosing scope and methods for the question,
  monitoring gaps, synthesizing multiple sources, understanding authority as contextual, and treating
  search as nonlinear strategic exploration.
- [Cochrane Handbook, Chapter 4: Searching for and selecting studies](https://training.cochrane.org/handbook/current/chapter-04)
  — evidence-review basis for planned eligibility, source selection, sensitive search, search
  documentation, corrections/retractions, deduplication, and distinguishing studies from multiple
  reports. These requirements are deliberately limited to rigorous review mode.
- [PRISMA 2020](https://www.prisma-statement.org/prisma-2020) and its
  [checklist](https://www.prisma-statement.org/prisma-2020-checklist) — basis for transparent
  systematic-review reporting, including objectives, eligibility, information sources, and selection.
  PRISMA itself says its main guideline is for systematic reviews; the package therefore warns
  against applying it to every lookup.

## Source evaluation

- Wineburg, Breakstone, McGrew, Smith, and Ortega's 2022
  [district-wide lateral-reading study](https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000740), summarized by the
  [Digital Inquiry Group](https://cor.inquirygroup.org/research/lateral-reading-on-the-open-internet/)
  and indexed by [ERIC](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1372738) — empirical basis for leaving an
  unfamiliar site to investigate it elsewhere. High-school government teachers in one urban
  district delivered six lessons; matched treatment classrooms (`n=271`) were compared with regular
  classrooms (`n=228`) using repeated-measures analysis. Students in the intervention improved
  their digital-credibility judgements, but that population and classroom intervention do not by
  themselves establish the same effect for every age, setting, or autonomous agent.
- [Digital Inquiry Group: Lateral Reading Poster](https://cor.inquirygroup.org/curriculum/lessons/lateral-reading-poster/?cuid=teaching-lateral-reading)
  — concise practitioner-facing account of the professional fact-checker behavior behind lateral
  reading.
- [Mike Caulfield, SIFT: The Four Moves](https://hapgood.us/2019/06/19/sift-the-four-moves/)
  — practitioner source for stop, investigate the source, find better coverage, and trace claims to
  original context. The package expands the moves with claim-fitness checks rather than copying the
  teaching material.
- [Cochrane Handbook section 4.4.6](https://training.cochrane.org/handbook/current/chapter-04#section-4-4-6)
  — basis for checking errata, corrections, expressions of concern, and retractions in rigorous
  evidence work.

## Citation and synthesis

- [Purdue OWL plagiarism overview](https://owl.purdue.edu/owl/avoiding_plagiarism/documents/plagiarism_one_pager.pdf)
  — basis for recording sources during research and attributing others' words and ideas.
- [Purdue Global: Basic Documentation Guidelines](https://purdueglobalwriting.center/basic-documentation-guidelines-2/)
  — practitioner guidance for accurate paraphrase, sparse quotation, and citation of borrowed
  wording or interpretation.
- [Crossref display guidelines](https://www.crossref.org/display-guidelines/) and
  [researcher overview](https://www.crossref.org/community/researchers) — basis for preferring a DOI
  link as a persistent identifier for research works when one is available.
- [ACRL Framework: Research as Inquiry](https://www.ala.org/acrl/standards/ilframework#research)
  — basis for organizing information meaningfully, synthesizing sources, drawing reasonable
  conclusions, and keeping disagreement visible as part of the scholarly conversation.

## Good/bad pairs

- Search-snippet, repeated-reporting, lateral-reading, and upstream-tracing pairs apply the Digital
  Inquiry Group and SIFT findings to an agent's evidence workflow.
- Source-by-source narration and unsupported compound-claim pairs apply ACRL synthesis practices and
  Purdue attribution guidance. They are minimized examples of the documented failure, not generated
  factual claims.
- Selective-versus-systematic and study-versus-report pairs apply the Cochrane and PRISMA contracts.
- Vendor benchmark and peer-review pairs are bounded examples of ACRL's rule that authority depends
  on context; they do not rank source categories universally.
