# Customer research source basis

This package is a Rundesk synthesis of professional survey standards, peer-reviewed work on response
and selection bias, qualitative-methods literature, a federal rule on consumer reviews, and the
industry code governing contact with research participants. Use this file to audit or update any
claim in the other references.

**Read in this order of authority.** A professional standard or regulation states a requirement.
Peer-reviewed work measures an effect. Institutional research documents practice. Practitioner
writing is an informed position. Never present a later tier as an earlier one.

Verified against the sources listed here in **August 2026**. Several sources below were read as
downloaded documents rather than rendered pages because the publishers block automated clients;
where a claim is quoted, it came from the document text.

## Tier 1 — professional standards and regulation

- [AAPOR Standard Definitions, 10th edition](https://aapor.org/standards-and-ethics/standard-definitions/)
  ([PDF](http://aapor.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/Standards-Definitions-10th-edition.pdf)): the
  case-disposition vocabulary, the family of response-rate formulas from minimum to maximum, and the
  instruction that the eligibility estimate must not be selected to boost the rate. Also that a
  response rate alone does not establish whether nonresponse error exists.
- [AAPOR Code of Professional Ethics and Practices](https://aapor.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/AAPOR-2020-Code_FINAL_APPROVED.pdf)
  and [disclosure standards](https://aapor.org/standards-and-ethics/disclosure-standards/): the
  prohibition on conducting non-research activity under the guise of research, voluntary
  participation, the identifiability protection, and the disclosure list a published result must
  satisfy — including probability versus non-probability sample declaration and the limits on
  reporting precision for non-probability samples.
- [AAPOR statement on push polls](https://aapor.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/Push-Poll-508.pdf):
  the diagnostic features of a persuasion instrument disguised as research, and the explicit refusal
  to treat negative content alone as disqualifying.
- [ICC/ESOMAR International Code, July 2025](https://standards.esomar.org/assets/documents/icc-esomar-code-2025.pdf):
  the research/non-research distinction and separate consent for non-research purposes; researcher
  identification; voluntary participation on clear information; re-contact agreement; retention
  statement; withdrawal and deletion; and the requirement to disclose a synthetic persona conducting
  data collection.
- [16 CFR Part 465, Rule on the Use of Consumer Reviews and Testimonials](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-D/part-465)
  — [Federal Register, 89 FR 68034, 22 August 2024](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2024-08-22/pdf/2024-18519.pdf),
  rule text at 89 FR 68077, **effective 21 October 2024**: the prohibited practices, including
  compensation conditioned on a review expressing a particular sentiment, insider reviews without
  disclosure, company-controlled review sites, and review suppression — and the carve-out for
  generalized solicitations to purchasers.
- [45 CFR Part 46](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CFR-2023-title45-vol1/pdf/CFR-2023-title45-vol1-part46.pdf):
  the exemption criteria for interview and survey procedures, used here as a structural risk test
  rather than as a claim that ordinary product research is regulated by it.

## Tier 1 — institutional research on survey method

- [Pew Research Center, writing survey questions](https://www.pewresearch.org/writing-survey-questions/):
  the split-sample wording, open-versus-closed, and question-order results quoted in
  `asking-people.md`, plus acquiescence and social-desirability direction.
- [Comparing two types of online survey samples (2023)](https://www.pewresearch.org/methods/2023/09/07/comparing-two-types-of-online-survey-samples/):
  opt-in error roughly twice that of probability-based panels, and the subgroup error magnitudes.
- [Assessing the risks to online polls from bogus respondents (2020)](https://www.pewresearch.org/methods/2020/02/18/assessing-the-risks-to-online-polls-from-bogus-respondents/):
  the 4-7% prevalence, trace levels in address-recruited panels, and that bogus respondents skew
  positive and therefore bias rather than add noise.
- [Evaluating online nonprobability surveys (2016)](https://www.pewresearch.org/methods/2016/05/02/evaluating-online-nonprobability-surveys/):
  that a representative demographic profile does not predict accuracy.

## Tier 3 — practitioner positions, cited as such

- [Christensen Institute on jobs-to-be-done](https://www.christenseninstitute.org/theory/jobs-to-be-done/):
  the framework's own framing as a lens — and, by its silence, the absence of a stated evidence base
  or methodology.
- [HBS Working Knowledge on the milkshake case](https://www.library.hbs.edu/working-knowledge/clay-christensens-milkshake-marketing):
  the actual method — one day of observation in one restaurant plus exit interviews, number
  unstated — and the argument that demographic membership correlates with rather than causes a
  purchase.
- [Nielsen Norman Group on participation inequality](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/participation-inequality/):
  the 90-9-1 formulation and that a company reading web postings gets an unrepresentative sample.
  **Practitioner observation, not peer-reviewed.**
- Yankelovich & Meer, *Harvard Business Review* 84(2), 2006, [landing page](https://hbr.org/2006/02/rediscovering-market-segmentation):
  the 1964 introduction of non-demographic segmentation and the argument that demographic and
  psychographic approaches have both become weak predictors. **Managerial writing; body paywalled.**
- Haley 1968 (benefit segmentation) and Dolnicar 2004 (a priori versus data-driven) are named as the
  provenance of those ideas. **Neither text was opened**; they are cited for existence and
  attribution only, and nothing is quoted from them.

## What this package deliberately does not cite

- **Jobs-to-be-done as validated method.** No peer-reviewed evaluation was found, and the package
  says so instead of implying one exists.
- **The commonly quoted jobs-to-be-done definition**, which could not be verified against its
  paywalled source article.
- **Outcome-driven innovation's claimed success rates**, which are vendor claims.
- **A star rating average** as a quality or satisfaction estimate.
- **A persona** as evidence, or with a population attached.
- **A community thread** as evidence of prevalence.
- **GDPR article text**, which could not be retrieved; the package refers to lawful basis as a
  question for a privacy owner rather than quoting a provision it did not read.
