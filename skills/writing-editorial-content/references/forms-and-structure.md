# Choose and shape the editorial form

Form is a promise about what the reader will receive. Use these shapes as starting hypotheses, then
adapt them to the assignment, evidence, publication, and voice.

## Blog post

A blog is a publishing channel, not one structure. Choose the actual editorial job: announce,
explain, teach, analyze, reflect, report, persuade, or tell a story.

Make the product or organization's reason for speaking visible. A useful post contributes firsthand
experience, original reporting, a defensible analysis, a concrete explanation, or a distinctive
point of view. Do not manufacture a generic article around a search phrase and call the channel its
purpose.

## Development log

Anchor the piece to a real interval and state:

```text
Starting point and intended change
Constraint, uncertainty, or decision that shaped the work
What was attempted and why
What was observed, learned, changed, rejected, or left unresolved
Evidence: implementation, test, measurement, artifact, or direct account
Current state: explored / decided / built / tested / shipped, using the product's real terms
What follows, using only approved plans and keeping them separate from accomplished work
```

Use enough technical detail to make the decisions and learning credible for the audience. Link to
reference documentation rather than turning the devlog into it. Do not turn normal work into a
heroic narrative, hide failed paths that explain the decision, or describe a local build as shipped.
Do not invent the next step, rollout path, owner, deadline, or promise of another update.
Audit verbs that imply work continues or a future action is committed. A failing test establishes a
result, not automatically that a team `is investigating`, `will fix`, or `will report back`.

## Reported or explanatory article

Establish the focus early enough that readers understand the subject and significance. The opening
may lead with the result, a scene, a question, a contradiction, a character, or a concrete detail;
the following context must reveal what the piece is actually about and why it matters.

Organize by the reader's reasoning path rather than by the order research was collected. Useful
structures include:

- importance first, then explanation and consequence;
- question, evidence, answer, and limits;
- problem, mechanism, response, and unresolved boundary;
- chronological change when sequence explains causality or experience;
- compare and contrast against fixed criteria; and
- scene and context alternating around one story question.

A `nut graf`, theme statement, or central-question note can help the writer find the focus. It is not
a compulsory paragraph or placement. Delete the drafting device when the published structure makes
the promise more gracefully.

## Column or essay

A column earns its first person and recurring voice through judgment, not self-reference. State one
arguable thesis, show how the author arrived there, use evidence fairly, engage the strongest
relevant counterweight, and make clear where reported fact ends and interpretation begins.

Do not disguise promotion as independent commentary or opinion as settled fact. A column can be
subjective without being careless: quotations, numbers, descriptions of others, and product claims
remain verifiable.

## Narrative or brand story

Build from truthful particulars: people, place, goal, obstacle, decision, change, and consequence.
Use scenes only when the details are observed, recorded, or supplied by an accountable source. Make
reconstruction visible. Never invent dialogue, interior thought, chronology, composite characters,
or sensory detail.

Story structure should reveal meaning rather than impose false drama. A small honest change can be
the arc. Not every story needs suspense, a villain, a three-act template, or a triumphant ending.

## Titles, openings, and endings

Draft the title after the focus is stable, even if a working title came first. A strong title is
specific, concise enough for its channel, true at ordinary reading, and distinct from neighboring
pieces. Do not hide a weak premise behind shock, mystery, a number, or an unsupported superlative.

The opening should create orientation, interest, or momentum without delaying the subject for
ceremony. Cut throat-clearing about the importance of technology, change, modern life, or a dictionary
definition unless that framing is itself the point.

The ending should resolve, complicate, return, or point forward from the established center. Do not
repeat the introduction mechanically, add a sales pitch the piece did not earn, or introduce the
main insight after the evidence is over.

## Format for the channel

Use meaningful headings when they help navigation; do not interrupt a short narrative only to meet a
heading quota. Keep heading levels hierarchical. Use lists for genuinely parallel items, tables for
comparisons readers will scan across, and prose for argument, explanation, and story.

Use descriptive link text, introduce media where its purpose matters, provide alt-text requirements,
and keep meaning independent of decoration. A screenshot or chart needs the fact the reader should
notice, not `image of dashboard`.
