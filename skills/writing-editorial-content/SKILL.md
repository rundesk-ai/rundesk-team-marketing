# Write editorial content

Make the piece worth a particular reader's time before making it sound polished. Strong grammar
cannot rescue an empty premise, borrowed point of view, unsupported story, or format chosen by habit.

## Establish the editorial contract

Record the brief before drafting:

```text
Artifact and channel:
Owner and named author:
Audience and reading situation:
Reader's current knowledge, questions, stakes, and vocabulary:
Purpose and useful change for the reader:
Central point or story question:
Product facts and approved direction:
Evidence, experience, quotations, and source limits:
Author voice, product voice, tone, and English variant:
Required format, length boundary, links, media, and metadata:
Claim, legal, privacy, approval, placement, and publication boundaries:
Acceptance check:
```

Distinguish `not supplied` from `absent`. If the audience, product truth, authorial authority, central
point, or evidence needed for the requested piece is missing, ask for it or return a bounded outline
with the gap visible. Do not fabricate a reader, customer result, quotation, scene, anecdote, product
decision, release state, planned action, future communication, commitment, or personal experience to
make the draft feel complete.

Read [voice and audience](references/voice-and-audience.md) when the request names a character,
founder, executive, product voice, house style, unfamiliar audience, or examples to match.

## Find the piece before writing it

Turn a topic into one defensible editorial center:

```text
This piece is for <reader> who needs or cares about <situation>.
It establishes <specific point, change, or story> through <evidence or experience>.
It earns attention because <reader value now>.
It does not claim <nearest unsupported or out-of-scope conclusion>.
```

An assignment such as `write about the launch` names a topic, not a piece. Establish what changed,
why it matters to this audience, what the author can credibly say, and what is newly useful or
original. Prefer a narrower piece with substance over a comprehensive summary that says nothing new.
If the approved evidence cannot support the requested length or form, return the strongest shorter
piece or an evidence-shaped outline and name the shortfall. Never pad with generic advice, plausible
consequences, invented examples, or repeated conclusions to hit a count.

Use the approved thesis, theme, or story question as an editing test, not a sentence every article
must print. Every major section should advance, complicate, evidence, or resolve it. Cut material
that merely proves the writer did research.

## Choose the form by its reader promise

Read [forms and structure](references/forms-and-structure.md) before shaping a blog post, development
log, reported article, column, essay, or story. Choose by purpose, evidence, author stance, and reader
expectation—not by a universal template.

At minimum, make these distinctions:

| Form | Reader promise | Required discipline |
|---|---|---|
| Blog post | A useful idea, update, explanation, or perspective from the publishing organization | Earn the post with product knowledge, experience, or analysis rather than generic topic coverage |
| Development log | What was attempted, learned, changed, or remains unresolved in a real development interval | Separate observed work from interpretation, plans, and shipped state; preserve technical truth without becoming reference documentation |
| Article | A focused explanation or report that helps a reader understand a subject | Establish the central point, context, evidence, attribution, and why it matters |
| Column | A recurring author's argued interpretation or perspective | Make the thesis, reasoning, evidence, counterweight, and authorial stance clear; label commentary rather than presenting opinion as reported fact |
| Narrative or brand story | A truthful change experienced through people, scenes, decisions, or consequences | Use only established details and quotations; never manufacture drama, dialogue, motive, or composite experience |

The title, opening, structure, and ending must make the same promise. Do not use a dramatic opening
that the body abandons, a headline stronger than the evidence, or a conclusion that introduces the
actual point for the first time.

## Draft from evidence and voice

Build an evidence ledger for every material factual, comparative, quantified, attributed, or
experience claim:

```text
Claim | source or authority | direct fact / interpretation / opinion | wording limit | citation or approval
```

Use primary and original sources when available. Attribute others' facts, language, ideas, and
reported experience. Quote only words verified against the source, preserve their meaning and
context, and never convert paraphrase into quotation. Remove an unsupported claim or quotation from
a finished piece rather than making its lack of verification part of the published prose; keep the
gap in editorial notes unless the piece is explicitly about that uncertainty. Label commentary,
disclosed first-person experience, projections, and unknowns for what they are.

A familiar-sounding domain generalization is still a claim. Do not add one as connective tissue or
substance merely because it seems plausible; establish it, attribute it, present it as authorized
opinion, or omit it.

Write from the selected voice model, but preserve truth and readability over performance. Voice is
the stable relationship among point of view, stance, vocabulary, syntax, rhythm, humor, metaphor,
and degree of formality; it is not a bag of catchphrases. Tone adapts that voice to the subject and
reader's situation. Do not copy distinctive phrases or exaggerate quirks until the author becomes a
caricature.

Treat voice samples as evidence of language behavior, never as sentences for the new piece. Labels
such as `approved voice sample`, `match this voice`, or `preserve this voice` establish provenance
but do not authorize reuse. Reuse exact wording only when the owner explicitly says to quote or use
that line in the piece; otherwise reproduce the supported patterns in original prose.

Draft in the requested English variant and house style. When neither is supplied, use consistent
plain international English and flag the unresolved editorial choice instead of mixing variants.
Choose exact words the audience understands; specialist vocabulary is right when the audience uses
it and wrong when it performs expertise for them.

## Revise in the right order

Read [editing and language](references/editing-and-language.md) before reviewing a draft, applying
house style, correcting grammar, or formatting for the web.

Use separate passes so later polish does not hide an earlier failure:

1. **Truth and authority:** facts, quotations, attribution, claims, authorial authority, approvals.
2. **Editorial substance:** audience value, central point, originality, evidence, missing reporting.
3. **Structure:** opening promise, sequence, emphasis, transitions, pace, ending, title alignment.
4. **Voice and line:** point of view, tone, diction, rhythm, concrete detail, sentence and paragraph work.
5. **Copy edit:** grammar, usage, punctuation, spelling, capitalization, numbers, names, English variant, house consistency.
6. **Proof and format:** headings, links, lists, quotations, media notes, metadata, rendering, final typos.

Read the piece aloud during the line pass. Review headings as an outline and the opening sentence of
each paragraph as a second outline. These expose repeated cadence, missing logic, buried emphasis,
and sections that do not carry their share of the argument or story.

Do not treat a readability score, grammar checker, search score, or successful spellcheck as a
quality verdict. Each is a diagnostic with a narrow oracle. A deliberate fragment can serve voice;
an immaculate sentence can still be false, dull, or aimed at nobody.

A checker recommendation is not a house or publication requirement merely because a tool reports
it. Apply an exact count, grade, voice ratio, or sentence rule only when the brief gives that rule
editorial authority. Otherwise use the result to inspect candidates, explain any material conflict,
and choose by truth, audience, voice, and meaning.

## Return the editorial package

Before returning, audit the draft sentence by sentence against the evidence ledger. Verify every
past, present, ongoing, and future action as well as every claim, quotation, and first-person
statement. Delete language such as `we are testing`, `we will share`, `users need not act`, or a
promised next step when the source establishes only a current artifact or result. Grammatical tense
is not evidence.

For a creation request, return:

1. the finished piece in the requested format;
2. title, dek or summary, byline/date, excerpt, and link or media notes only when the channel needs them;
3. the interpreted audience, purpose, central point, voice, English variant, and format;
4. sources and claim or approval notes; and
5. remaining decisions or evidence gaps.

For a review, lead with the revised piece when rewriting was requested. Otherwise return ranked
findings separated into truth/authority, substance, structure, voice/line, copy, and format, with
specific examples and fixes. Preserve intentional voice; do not flatten every author into generic
corporate prose.

Return content as text unless the request explicitly authorizes writing to a named destination.
Drafting authority is not placement, publication, scheduling, distribution, or sending authority.
