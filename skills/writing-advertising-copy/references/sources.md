# Advertising-copy source map

Use this file to audit a lesson, not as a second procedure. Platform specifications and policies
change; verify current mechanics when they matter. Verified 26 August 2026 against the linked pages.

## Audience relevance, keywords, assets, and destinations

- [Google Ads Help: Write compelling, genuine ad copy](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6167101?hl=en-AU)
  recommends focusing on user needs and benefits and showing value without becoming pushy. It
  supports the audience-and-offer center; its examples are not universal conversion claims.
- [Google Ads Help: Create messaging that reflects your brand](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6167114?hl=en)
  recommends finding what is compelling about the business, building ads around it, and keeping
  messaging consistent with the brand. It supports treating voice and offer as linked controls.
- [Google Ads Help: How to create a relevant ad](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/14998307?hl=en)
  connects responsive-search assets, landing pages, existing ads, keyword themes, and query
  relevance. It supports the query-to-destination message path without making keyword inclusion a
  guarantee of results.
- [Google Ads Help: About responsive search ads](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7684791?hl=en-0)
  explains that multiple headlines and descriptions are recombined and should make sense
  individually and together. It is the basis for modular asset review; current field limits must be
  rechecked rather than memorized.
- [Google Ads Help: Set up keyword insertion](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6371157?hl=en)
  identifies real insertion failures including length, nonsense, grammar, spelling, landing-page,
  restricted-content, and trademark problems. The adversarial substitution audit turns those
  platform failure classes into a writing check.
- [Google Ads Help: About Ad Strength](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/9921843?hl=en)
  says Ad Strength evaluates asset variety and relevance but does not determine eligibility, Ad
  Rank, Quality Score, auction wins, landing-page quality, or offer quality. It supports using the
  score diagnostically rather than as a writing verdict.
- [Microsoft Advertising: Search ads](https://about.ads.microsoft.com/en/solutions/ad-products-formats/search)
  independently confirms that responsive search ads recombine distinct headlines and descriptions
  for relevant searches and that the advertiser still controls copy, destinations, and offers. It
  supports combination-safe variety and message match; Microsoft's optimization language is a
  platform description, not proof that any drafted asset will perform.

## Paid social and sponsored content

- [Meta for Business: Instagram and Facebook Reels ads](https://www.facebook.com/business/ads/facebook-instagram-reels-ads)
  treats Reels ads as a distinct mobile placement, recommends keeping key messages in safe zones,
  and describes A/B testing of native creative. Its performance figures are Meta studies with
  specified conditions, not universal promises copied into advertiser claims.
- [Pinterest Business Help: Review ad specs](https://help.pinterest.com/en/business/article/pinterest-product-specs)
  documents format-specific titles, descriptions, overlays, media, and safe zones and says
  descriptions help relevance. It supports verifying the exact Pin-ad schema and limits at drafting
  time rather than treating one spec as permanent.
- [Pinterest Business Help: Pin performance and distribution](https://help.pinterest.com/en/business/article/pin-performance-and-distribution)
  recommends relevant keywords, contextual titles and descriptions, and alignment among creative,
  copy, and destination. These are platform recommendations, not guaranteed distribution outcomes.

## Claims, disclosure, and editorial quality

- [Federal Trade Commission: Advertising and Marketing](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/advertising-marketing)
  states that advertising claims must be truthful, non-deceptive, fair, and evidence-based, with
  additional rules for some subjects. This package flags substantiation and review; it does not
  provide legal approval.
- [FTC: Disclosures 101 for Social Media Influencers](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/disclosures-101-social-media-influencers)
  says material connections should be disclosed clearly with the endorsement, where they are hard
  to miss, rather than hidden after `more` or inside hashtag groups. The exact legal requirement
  still depends on jurisdiction and context.
- [Google Ads policies](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/6008942?hl=en)
  requires clear, relevant, professional ads and destinations and rejects gimmicky or overly generic
  presentation. Platform approval remains separate from the writing review.
- [Google Ads misrepresentation policy](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/6020955?hl=en)
  rejects ads or destinations that deceive by omission or misleading product and business
  information. It supports reviewing the whole ad-to-destination promise.

## Catalog conclusions and limits

- The message spine, attraction angles, voice card, query-to-destination path, asset roles, and
  eight-pass audit are local copy controls assembled from the cited principles; no platform
  mandates those labels or sequence.
- Copy-side keyword mapping begins with approved query evidence. The package does not research
  demand, volume, competition, bids, targeting, or campaign structure and does not infer that a
  keyword will perform.
- No source establishes a universal best headline, emotional appeal, power-word list, character
  utilization rate, keyword density, asset count, call to action, or conversion result. Current
  platform rules, approved product evidence, audience evidence, and controlled results govern.
