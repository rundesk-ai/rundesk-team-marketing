# Editorial-writing source map

Use this file to audit a lesson, not as another writing procedure. The package combines official
content and accessibility guidance, journalism ethics, university writing instruction, and
experienced editorial practice. No source establishes a universal article template or engagement
formula.

Verified 26 August 2026 against the linked pages.

## Audience, purpose, voice, and originality

- [Purdue Online Writing Lab: The Writing Process](https://owl.purdue.edu/owl/resources/teaching_resources/documents/the-writing-process-20250724.pdf)
  begins planning with purpose, intended audience, and appropriate style or tone. It supports the
  editorial contract; its classroom process is not a mandatory production sequence.
- [Microsoft Writing Style Guide: Microsoft's brand voice](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/style-guide/brand-voice-above-all-simple-human)
  distinguishes message from voice and stable voice from context-dependent tone. Its specific warm,
  relaxed Microsoft personality is an example, not a default copied into another product.
- [Mailchimp Content Style Guide: Voice and tone](https://styleguide.mailchimp.com/voice-and-tone/)
  likewise treats voice as relatively stable and tone as responsive to the reader's state. Its
  humor, informality, and house preferences belong to Mailchimp; this package adapts the distinction,
  not the persona.
- [Mailchimp: Writing goals and principles](https://styleguide.mailchimp.com/writing-principles/)
  starts useful content with purpose, audience, and reader need, values clarity over promotion, and
  warns against overclaiming. It supports writing for the reader rather than marketing at them.
- [Google Search Central: Creating helpful, reliable, people-first content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content)
  asks whether content supplies original information, reporting, research, or analysis; serves an
  intended audience; demonstrates firsthand expertise; avoids factual and stylistic defects; and
  identifies who created it. It explicitly rejects preferred word-count folklore and search-first
  production. These are useful self-review questions, not proof that a page will rank.
- [Google Search: Optimizing for generative AI features](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide)
  recommends non-commodity, useful, reliable content with a unique point of view rather than many
  query variations or recycled summaries. It supports the originality test; it does not make any
  single structure or voice an AI-search requirement.

## Structure, language, and accessible web form

- [GOV.UK: Use the right tone](https://guidance.publishing.service.gov.uk/writing-to-gov-uk-standards/writing-guidelines/right-tone/)
  recommends specific, informative, clear, concise, brisk but human writing and picturing the
  audience. Its emotionless public-service tone is not a universal editorial voice.
- [GOV.UK: Use clear language](https://guidance.publishing.service.gov.uk/writing-to-gov-uk-standards/writing-guidelines/clear-language/)
  supports plain language, short chunks, and active voice when it puts the reader and action first,
  while naming cases where passive voice better serves the result or user. Its numerical sentence
  and paragraph targets are diagnostics for that service, not universal quality gates.
- [Mailchimp: Grammar and mechanics](https://styleguide.mailchimp.com/grammar-and-mechanics/)
  supports information hierarchy, descriptive headings, concise and specific language, and internal
  consistency. Its serial comma and other mechanics are house choices, which is why this package
  follows the requested house style and English variant first.
- [W3C Web Accessibility Initiative: Writing for web accessibility](https://www.w3.org/WAI/tips/writing/)
  supports informative titles, meaningful heading structure, descriptive link text, useful text
  alternatives, clear instructions, and concise content. These are accessibility and navigation
  practices, not a quota for headings or lists.
- [Google developer documentation style guide: Write accessible documentation](https://developers.google.com/style/accessibility)
  adds concrete guidance on semantic headings, meaningful links, parallel lists, important
  information early, and keeping meaning independent of visual formatting. The package applies those
  web-surface lessons to editorial formatting without taking ownership of technical documentation.

## Reporting, claims, and story focus

- [Society of Professional Journalists Code of Ethics](https://www.spj.org/spj-code-of-ethics/)
  calls for verifying before release, using original sources when possible, supplying context,
  identifying sources, labeling advocacy and commentary, avoiding distortion, never plagiarizing,
  correcting promptly, and distinguishing news from advertising. SPJ states that the code is a set
  of ethical principles, not enforceable law; this package applies its accuracy discipline
  proportionately to reported and branded editorial content.
- [Federal Trade Commission: Advertising and Marketing](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/advertising-marketing)
  states that advertising claims must be truthful, non-deceptive, and evidence-based, with additional
  rules for some subjects. Editorial content can still function as advertising; this package flags
  approval and substantiation but does not provide legal approval.
- [Nieman Storyboard: Getting to the heart of the story](https://niemanstoryboard.org/2021/10/19/nut-grafs-getting-to-the-heart-of-the-story/)
  describes a theme statement or functional `nut` as a way to find the meaning that guides reporting,
  structure, inclusion, and emphasis. This is experienced practitioner guidance, not a controlled
  comparison or a requirement to print one summary paragraph.
- [Nieman Storyboard: Nut grafs—overused, misused, or misunderstood?](https://niemanstoryboard.org/nut-grafs-overused-misused-or-merely-misunderstood/)
  documents both the reader-orientation value and the risk that a forced nut interrupts narrative
  voice. It supports treating focus as required and the visible device as optional.
- [Purdue Online Writing Lab: Journalism and Journalistic Writing](https://owl.purdue.edu/owl/subject_specific_writing/journalism_and_journalistic_writing/index.html)
  distinguishes news, features, reviews, columns, and other journalistic forms and supports choosing
  structure for the form and reader rather than treating every article alike. Newsroom conventions
  remain publication choices, not universal branded-content requirements.

## Revision and editing

- [Purdue Online Writing Lab: Revising and Editing](https://owl.purdue.edu/owl/graduate_writing/introduction_to_writing/documents/revising-and-editing/quick-tips-for-revising-and-editing.pdf)
  distinguishes higher-order revision of claims, organization, and sections from later editing of
  sentence structure, grammar, citations, wordiness, and typographical errors. It supports ordering
  substantive revision before copy editing while acknowledging that genre conventions differ.

## Catalog conclusions and limits

- `Blog post`, `development log`, `article`, `column`, and `narrative` are separated by reader
  promise, evidence, and author stance so one package can route related editorial work without
  pretending every publication uses the same template.
- The blog-and-article workflow turns the cited audience, people-first, reporting, focus,
  accessibility, and revision lessons into an assignment, reporting map, article architecture, and
  acceptance audit. Those named artifacts are local editorial controls, not claims that all
  publications use one production system.
- The voice card turns the stable-voice/contextual-tone distinction into observable language
  features. No source validates imitation from one sample, so the package requires representative
  approved evidence and treats missing voice as unknown.
- The staged edit combines Purdue's higher/later-order distinction with the accuracy, voice,
  accessibility, and final-surface checks above. Its six pass names are catalog organization, not an
  empirical claim that every newsroom uses those labels.
- No linked source establishes a universal best headline pattern, word count, sentence length,
  paragraph count, keyword density, heading frequency, reading grade, narrative arc, or conversion
  result. The package rejects those as universal rules and follows supported audience, channel, and
  house constraints instead.
