# Plan and create Pinterest content

Use this playbook after the owner has chosen Pinterest, the audience, the business objective, and
the monetization path. It owns the organic content plan and Pin artifacts. It does not choose channel
investment, analyze an account, operate Pinterest, build a product feed, set up tracking, negotiate a
partnership, or promise reach or revenue.

## Model the traffic path before making Pins

Treat Pinterest as visual discovery, search, planning, saving, and shopping—not only as a follower
feed. Map the complete path:

```text
eligible impression -> useful Pin encounter -> save, closeup, profile visit, or follow
-> outbound click -> destination action -> qualified value or revenue
```

Choose one primary outcome per portfolio or experiment. Keep its metric distinct from adjacent
signals:

| Objective | Primary evidence | Do not substitute |
|---|---|---|
| Discovery | impressions and total audience for the named population and window | follower count |
| Return-worthy utility | saves and save rate | impressions |
| On-Pinterest interaction | engagements and engagement rate | comments alone |
| Website traffic | outbound clicks and outbound-click rate | Pin clicks, which can stop at closeup |
| Repeat audience | follows, profile visits, and later reach from the defined content promise | one viral Pin |
| Commercial value | attributed destination conversions, qualified outcomes, and realized value | clicks or product views |

Pinterest Analytics defines these platform metrics, but an impression, save, Pin click, or outbound
click does not establish a sale or causal effect. Ask the measurement owner for a dated baseline,
equal-age cohorts, destination analytics, conversion evidence, and revenue reconciliation. If only
impressions exist, report traffic and monetization as unestablished.

Current platform audience figures can help with channel consideration, but they age quickly and are
usually reported or commissioned by Pinterest. Verify the date, geography, population, and source
before using one. Never turn a global audience statistic into evidence that the named buyer is
reachable or will convert.

## Build a search- and planning-led portfolio

Start from evidenced searches, audience language, planning moments, destination inventory, and the
approved commercial path. Give every Pin one search or recommendation context, one useful promise,
one content job, and one destination. Use a mix only when each lane has a defined role:

| Lane | Audience job | Suitable source or destination |
|---|---|---|
| Evergreen utility | learn, compare, make, organize, or return later | durable guide, tutorial, recipe, checklist, video, or tool |
| Seasonal or life moment | plan before a predictable decision or event | timely guide, collection, service, or product page |
| Product in context | see what the item is, how it fits, or how it is used | accurate product or category page |
| Decision support | narrow options by need, style, use, recipient, or constraint | comparison, buying guide, curated collection, or service page |
| Brand or creator authority | recognize a repeatable point of view or expertise | substantive article, demonstration, portfolio, or profile |

Use Pinterest Trends or supplied search evidence to identify when interest begins, peaks, and cools
for the exact market. Publish seasonal content at the demand lift-off, often weeks or months before
the calendar date, rather than waiting for the event. Keep evergreen material circulating between
peaks. A trend is usable only when the account can add relevant value; do not bend an unrelated
product or phrase around it.

Do not prescribe a universal product-to-editorial ratio. Product content serves a person already
evaluating an item. Utility and inspiration can create discovery, saves, trust, and an honest bridge
to a monetized destination. Build the ratio from the approved funnel, available destinations,
inventory, season, and observed conversion path. When those inputs are absent, offer distinct
portfolio options instead of inventing a percentage.

## Choose the format for the job

Verify current product behavior before production because Pinterest formats change.

| Format | Use it when | Guardrail |
|---|---|---|
| Single image Pin | one product, idea, result, or destination must be recognized immediately | default to one focal promise, not a miniature brochure |
| Video Pin | motion, a demonstration, transformation, or ordered sequence carries the value | make the cover and opening understandable; supply captions or transcript needs |
| Multi-asset video Pin | a current mobile workflow stitches several supplied assets into a sequence | write it as a video sequence, not a swipeable image gallery |
| Separate image Pins | different images, queries, audiences, or angles deserve independent discovery | make each variant substantively fresh; do not upload duplicate combinations |
| Product Pin | current price, availability, and an exact purchasable item reduce shopping ambiguity | require accurate synced metadata and a matching product destination |
| Collections Pin | one hero scene can lead to several coherent, tagged products | keep the collection and destination logic consistent and shoppable |
| Carousel | several cards need separate chapters, products, features, or destinations | currently paid; route copy to the advertising workflow and leave operation out |

For a “gallery versus single image” request, first identify the real decision. Use one image when one
visual can carry the promise. Use a sequence when order or comparison is essential. Use separate Pins
when the aim is to test distinct search intents or creative concepts. Use a shoppable collection for
coherent products in a scene. Do not recommend a gallery merely to increase asset count.

Static versus video is an account- and category-level test, not a platform hierarchy. Large
observational data found image Pins dominated its high-performing sample, while individual creator
case studies report different winners. Treat those results as hypotheses; compare formats on the
same objective, destination class, topic, season, and equal-age window.

## Specify the visual before writing around it

Use a vertical 2:3 canvas as the production default for standard Pins, then verify current specs for
the exact format. Require high-resolution assets, solid lighting, clean framing, mobile legibility,
and enough contrast to understand the focal object. For commercial creative, show the product
clearly and, when useful, in a realistic setting; avoid abstract or generic lifestyle imagery that
hides what is being offered.

Choose raw photography, designed imagery, collage, or text overlay by content job and category.
There is no universal winner. A recipe, checklist, tutorial, or article may need a concise overlay to
name the practical value. A visually self-explanatory product, destination, artwork, or room may be
stronger with less design. Test meaningfully different concepts instead of changing a font or color
and calling it a new idea.

On-Pin text must:

- make the subject and supported value recognizable at mobile size;
- use concrete audience language rather than generic hype;
- stay short enough to preserve the image and hierarchy;
- keep qualifications or disclosures with the claim they govern;
- avoid UI-obscured edges and fine-print dependency; and
- match what the image and destination actually deliver.

Keep branding recognizable but secondary to the idea. Supply alt text that explains the meaningful
visual information for a screen-reader user; never turn alt text into a hidden keyword list. If the
visual is absent, return image and alt-text requirements, not invented scene details.

## Align discovery copy and destination

Use one primary topic plus tightly related supporting language across the image, title, description,
board context, and destination:

- Put the specific subject and important qualifier early in the title.
- Use the description to add useful context, audience fit, and natural search language.
- Use the on-Pin promise to orient the person, not to repeat every metadata field.
- Choose a relevant board and related topics only when the brief authorizes them.
- Link to the exact useful page, item, collection, or action promised—not a generic home page.
- Check that the destination is reachable, mobile-usable, current, and consistent with the Pin.

There is no universal best description length, keyword count, hashtag count, or title formula. More
words can add mixed topical signals. Prefer the shortest complete description that makes the Pin and
destination easy to classify and trust. Do not stuff variants, tags, locations, or trending phrases
that the content does not serve.

Create fresh variants by changing a real creative concept, image, audience frame, use case, query,
or destination angle. The same destination can support several Pins when it genuinely fulfills each
promise. Repeating the same image, link, title, and description is duplication, not consistency, and
can trigger spam controls.

## Match content to the monetization route

Require one approved route and write the content around the value event:

| Route | Content bridge | Required control |
|---|---|---|
| Owned ecommerce | useful product, category, comparison, or collection content leading to purchase | current price, availability, item identity, landing-page match, and commerce measurement |
| Affiliate commission | original recommendation, demonstration, guide, or comparison leading to the exact eligible link | unique value, honest endorsement, clear nearby disclosure, and no traffic manipulation |
| Publisher revenue | useful Pin leading to substantive editorial content that earns ads or subscriptions | the destination must deliver the promised value without deceptive click inflation |
| Lead, service, or digital product | problem-, use-case-, or planning-led content leading to a relevant resource or offer | defined audience, qualified conversion, consent path, and downstream outcome evidence |
| Brand partnership | creator-true content for an already approved relationship | platform paid-partnership label plus any additional clear disclosure and approval |

Product Pins are not a replacement for useful organic content. They carry shopping facts and can be
created at scale from a catalog; catalog-created Product Pins can also support shopping ads. Product
rich Pins sync product data from a site but have different promotion rules. Keep the feed, Pin, and
landing page accurate. Building or uploading a catalog, claiming a site, installing a tag, applying
for merchant status, or starting an ad remains a separate authorized operation.

Affiliate and paid-partnership content is commercial content even when the Pin is organic. Require
an authentic presence, original value, truthful claims, and a disclosure ordinary people can notice
and understand near the endorsement. Do not hide the relationship in a hashtag block, rely solely on
a platform label, use fake accounts or saves, or manipulate traffic.

Do not promise “passive income,” virality, a revenue multiple, or a time-to-results. Practitioner and
platform case studies are selected examples with different audiences, assets, histories, offers, and
paid amplification. Use their repeated tactics as experiment candidates, never as a forecast.

## Plan cadence and timing without folklore

Create original content consistently at a sustainable quality level. Pinterest currently recommends
new original content at least weekly; that is a floor for planning, not proof that a particular daily
volume will work. Do not prescribe 5, 15, or 25 Pins a day, repin formulas, or a magic freshness
quota without current account evidence and a controlled reason.

There is no defensible universal best day or hour. Pinterest says Pins have no set engagement
window, and a large observational study found account audiences vary. Use:

1. the audience's geography and time zone;
2. Pinterest Trends for the topic's demand curve;
3. the account's equal-window performance by topic and format;
4. destination inventory and commercial deadlines; and
5. a sustainable production and review cadence.

Schedule for coverage and learning, not bursts of near-duplicates. Do not discard a Pin from its
first day: relevant Pins may gain engagement much later and resurface seasonally. Define early and
mature evaluation windows before launch, compare cohorts at the same age, and preserve seasonality
and format in the readout.

## Design for saves, follows, clicks, and trust

- **Earn saves** with material worth returning to: a recipe, checklist, reference, steps, options,
  or a plan. Use a save prompt only when saving is genuinely useful.
- **Earn follows** with a coherent, repeatable content promise and relevant boards. Ask for a follow
  only when the account can deliver that future value.
- **Earn outbound clicks** by making the destination's specific additional value obvious. Give
  enough value on the Pin to build trust; do not manufacture an information gap.
- **Earn comments** with a relevant decision, experience, or preference question. Do not use empty
  engagement bait. Drafting a prompt does not authorize replying.
- **Earn commercial action** by joining inspiration to a precise, trustworthy product or offer and
  reducing mismatch after the click.

Use comments, saves, follows, and clicks as feedback, not as permission to copy a winner endlessly.
Create adjacent value from the underlying audience need. Preserve negative evidence: high
impressions with weak outbound-click rate, strong saves with weak conversion, or high clicks with
poor destination outcomes are different problems.

## Return a decision-ready content package

When planning was requested, return:

```text
Approved business objective and monetization route
Audience, search intent, planning moment, and evidence
Primary metric, baseline status, and measurement handoff
Evergreen, seasonal, utility, product, and commercial lane choices with rationale
Pin briefs: format, image requirement, title, description, overlay, alt-text requirement, destination
Cadence and timing logic, including the trend lift-off or missing evidence
Fresh-variant and format experiment, with equal-age comparison windows
Disclosure, claim, product-data, accessibility, and publication requirements
Owner decisions, account operations, analytics, or evidence still needed
```

For a single Pin, return only the relevant fields. Read [sources](sources.md) before changing any
lesson in this playbook; it separates first-party mechanics, observational evidence, and selected
case studies.
