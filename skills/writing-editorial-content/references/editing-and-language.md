# Edit substance before sentences

Editing is a sequence of different judgments. Keep them separate enough that a clean copy edit does
not make an unresolved reporting or structural problem look finished.

## Diagnose before rewriting

State the draft's current contract in one sentence each: reader, purpose, central point, voice,
format, evidence, and desired reader effect. If those cannot be recovered from the draft or brief,
name the ambiguity before choosing one in the rewrite.

For each finding, use:

```text
Level: truth / substance / structure / voice-line / copy / proof-format
Observed text or pattern:
Reader consequence:
Recommended change:
Example only when it clarifies the fix:
Authority or decision needed:
```

Rank by consequence. A wrong claim outranks a slow paragraph. A missing point outranks comma style.

## Structural edit

Check whether the piece delivers one coherent promise to the intended reader:

- the title, opening, central point, evidence, and ending agree;
- essential context arrives before a reader needs it;
- each section has a distinct job and appears in the strongest sequence;
- examples and quotations advance the point rather than decorate it;
- assertions carry support proportional to their consequence;
- counterevidence, uncertainty, and limitations are represented fairly; and
- repetition, detours, background, and dead conclusions are cut.

Rewrite the outline before rewriting every sentence. Moving polished paragraphs repeatedly wastes
time and can preserve a structure that should have been replaced.

## Line edit for voice and force

Work paragraph by paragraph and sentence by sentence:

- put the actor, action, and consequence where readers can find them;
- prefer concrete nouns and precise verbs over adjective stacks and abstract nominalizations;
- vary sentence and paragraph length deliberately instead of generating one repeated cadence;
- make transitions express the actual relationship: sequence, contrast, cause, example, concession;
- cut throat-clearing, duplicated setup, filler intensifiers, empty superlatives, and summary that adds
  no meaning;
- preserve useful ambiguity, rhythm, humor, dialect, and fragments when they serve the approved
  voice and do not obscure meaning; and
- read aloud to catch false emphasis, breathless syntax, accidental rhyme, monotony, and language no
  person in this voice would say.

Active voice is a strong default when the actor matters. Passive voice is useful when the receiver,
result, or unknown actor is the point. Do not perform an automatic passive-to-active rewrite that
changes emphasis or invents responsibility.

## Copy edit for English and house consistency

Apply the requested house guide first. Otherwise establish and hold one English variant. Check:

- sentence boundaries, agreement, tense, pronoun reference, modifiers, parallelism, and idiom;
- spelling, hyphenation, compounds, capitalization, punctuation, quotation treatment, and possessives;
- names, titles, product terminology, version names, numbers, units, dates, and cited quotations;
- consistent treatment of headings, lists, emphasis, links, code, captions, and bylines; and
- inclusive and literal language appropriate to the audience, culture, accessibility needs, and
  translation path.

Do not confuse dialect or an intentional authorial register with error. Correctness is contextual:
published house style, quoted speech, a named English variant, and an approved voice can make forms
appropriate that a generic checker flags.

## Proof the final surface

Proof after structural and line changes are settled. Review both source and rendered form when tools
allow:

- title, dek, byline, dates, headings, paragraph breaks, and final call to action;
- broken, vague, adjacent, or misleading links;
- quotation marks, footnotes, citations, captions, alt text, and media placement;
- repeated, missing, doubled, or substituted words and final punctuation;
- mobile scanning, heading hierarchy, overflow, and copy stranded by templates; and
- metadata or excerpts that overstate or contradict the body.

Change the date only when the content was materially updated and follow the publication's policy.
Do not represent a copy edit as a new article.

## Reject mechanical quality proxies

Use spellcheck, grammar tools, readability scores, search tools, and style linters to find candidates
for human judgment. Reject their verdict when it conflicts with truth, audience vocabulary, approved
voice, accessibility, or the intended meaning.

Separate a tool's recommendation from an editor's authorized constraint. `The checker wants grade
six` is diagnostic context; `this publication requires 300 words` is a channel constraint when the
brief owner has authority to set it. Do not silently promote the first into the second. Do not add
generic claims, examples, or repetition to reach a target the evidence cannot support.

There is no universal best word count, sentence length, paragraph count, keyword density, heading
frequency, emotional arc, or reading-grade score for editorial quality. A constraint can be valid for
a channel or audience; it must be supplied or justified, not smuggled in as writing law.
