# Match the method to the question

Research quality depends on using enough process for the decision without burying a simple answer in
ceremony. Start with one mode and escalate when risk, disagreement, or missing evidence demands it.

## Narrow verification

Use this for a current value, exact behavior, attribution, version boundary, or other focused claim.

1. Rewrite the request as a claim that could be true, false, qualified, or unknown.
2. Identify the source responsible for that fact: the current law, official record, specification,
   source code, dataset, original study, or maintainer statement.
3. Open the exact source and check its date, version, scope, definitions, and exceptions.
4. Corroborate independently when the claim is contested, empirical, consequential, or surprising.
5. Return the answer, direct evidence, material caveat, and uncertainty.

```text
Bad:  find a recent article that quotes a product announcement
Good: read the current release notes and compatibility page; use independent testing for performance
```

Do not turn a direct contract lookup into a popularity poll. Multiple secondary pages do not outweigh
the current authoritative source for what a specification, law, or product promises.

## Broad landscape

Use this to understand a field, compare approaches, identify best practices, or prepare a briefing.

1. Map the question into subquestions, actors, vocabulary, source types, and likely disagreements.
2. Search divergently to learn alternate terms and locate seminal, current, and dissenting work.
3. Search convergently for the strongest evidence behind each emerging theme.
4. Follow citations backward to original work and forward to corrections, replications, and later
   interpretation.
5. Maintain the evidence ledger and a short gap list. Refine searches when a major perspective,
   affected group, jurisdiction, or failure mode is absent.
6. Synthesize themes, strength of evidence, disagreement, implications, and remaining gaps.

```text
Bad:  stop after the first page of results agrees with the initial framing
Good: vary terminology, identify who produces evidence, and deliberately search for limitations and
      credible disagreement before settling the themes
```

Stop when further credible searches no longer materially change the themes, not after an arbitrary
number of links. Saturation is a judgement; state meaningful exclusions and gaps.

## Rigorous or systematic review

Use only when the requested confidence, reproducibility, or stakes justify it. Domain-specific review
standards govern; a general web-search workflow is not a systematic review.

Before searching, record:

- a precise question and protocol;
- eligible source or study designs;
- inclusion and exclusion criteria;
- databases, repositories, sites, languages, jurisdictions, and dates;
- complete search strings and the final search date;
- screening, deduplication, extraction, and quality-assessment methods; and
- how disagreements, missing evidence, corrections, and retractions will be handled.

Document excluded candidates and reasons at the appropriate stage. Distinguish multiple reports of
one underlying study from independent studies. Consult an available, authorized domain specialist
or information professional when the method or decision warrants it; do not contact one without the
user's authority.

```text
Bad:  call a selective search "systematic" because it used many queries
Good: predeclare eligibility and sources, preserve reproducible searches, report selection, and use
      the reporting standard appropriate to the review type
```

PRISMA is a reporting guideline for systematic reviews, not a universal research checklist. Cochrane
methods are designed for evidence reviews and should not be presented as requirements for ordinary
technical or business research.
