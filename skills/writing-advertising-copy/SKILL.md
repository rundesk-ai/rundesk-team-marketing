# Write advertising copy

Make the right qualified person recognize a relevant, credible reason to act. Advertising copy is
not a pile of power words, a keyword container, or a promise made stronger than the product,
evidence, offer, or destination.

## Establish the advertising contract

Record:

```text
Advertiser, product, service, or offer being promoted:
Objective and approved conversion action:
Audience, awareness or intent state, need, desire, objection, and vocabulary:
Approved value proposition, differentiator, reason to believe, and proof:
Offer terms, price, eligibility, availability, geography, and dates:
Destination and the exact promise it fulfills:
Platform, placement, format, fields, current limits, and combination behavior:
Approved keywords, query themes, or audience language and their evidence:
Brand or author voice, tone, English variant, and mandatory wording:
Claims, comparisons, endorsements, disclosures, regulated limits, and approvals:
Variant hypothesis, success measure supplied by the experiment owner, and production boundary:
```

Distinguish `not supplied` from `none`. Do not invent an audience pain, benefit, differentiator,
price, promotion, urgency, scarcity, result, testimonial, guarantee, keyword demand, destination,
call to action, or approval. If the product or offer is not attractive on the supported facts,
return the missing evidence or a bounded message test rather than manufacturing desire.

Read [audience, offer, and voice](references/audience-offer-and-voice.md) to find the persuasive
center and preserve a distinctive voice. Read [keywords and message match](references/keywords-and-message-match.md)
for search ads, supplied keyword or query inputs, or discovery-led copy. Read [ad forms](references/ad-forms.md)
for platform fields, asset combinations, and paid social, search, display, or sponsored Pin copy.

## Build the message before the assets

Write one internal message spine:

```text
For <qualified audience in this moment>, <offer> provides <supported relevant value>
because <specific differentiator or proof>. <Approved action> leads to <matching destination>.
It does not promise <nearest unsupported conclusion>.
```

Then select the strongest supported attraction angle: recognized problem, desired outcome,
specific capability, concrete offer, credible proof, useful comparison, or timely approved reason.
Match it to awareness. Someone naming a product category needs clarity and difference; someone
naming a problem needs recognition and a credible bridge; someone naming the brand may need the
exact offer or next action. Do not pretend copy can repair a weak offer or mismatched destination.

Keep persuasion ethical. Use specificity, relevance, proof, and reduced uncertainty. Do not use
false urgency, shame, fear, identity pressure, manufactured consensus, disguised advertising,
misleading curiosity, or guarantees beyond evidence. Keep material qualifications and disclosures
where a person can notice and understand them; do not rely on the destination to cure a deceptive ad.

## Write modular, voice-true assets

Every asset must perform a job: identify the offer, mirror intent, express value, supply proof,
handle an objection, state a term, or invite the approved action. For systems that recombine assets,
each asset must remain grammatical, truthful, distinct, and compatible in likely combinations.
Do not generate a quota of synonym swaps or repeat the same claim across every headline.

Compression must preserve voice. Use the brand's approved stance, vocabulary, syntax, rhythm,
emotional temperature, and relationship to the audience. Let audience language improve recognition
without turning the brand into a search query or generic direct-response voice. Do not copy sample
phrases unless explicitly authorized. Voice samples establish language behavior, not product truth:
never turn their subject, metaphor, memorable word, or implied value into the ad's offer, benefit,
differentiator, proof, audience problem, or attraction angle unless another approved source does.

Use keywords to express message match, not to manufacture demand. Incorporate an approved primary
term where it makes the ad clearer and matches the destination; vary supporting assets across
benefit, proof, offer, objection, and action. Reject irrelevant high-volume terms, awkward exact
matches, stuffing, and dynamic insertion that can produce nonsense, false claims, or policy risk.

## Return a production-ready copy package

Return only fields the specified format uses, with current character counts when limits apply:

```text
Audience and intent
Offer and message spine
Platform / placement / format
Headlines, descriptions, primary text, on-creative copy, paths, or CTA labels
Keyword or query-theme mapping, when supplied
Destination and message-match notes
Claim, proof, qualification, disclosure, policy, and approval notes
Variant hypothesis and the one meaningful difference between treatments
Missing inputs and production boundaries
```

Verify current platform limits and policies from a first-party source or supplied live specification;
do not rely on remembered counts. Drafting never authorizes account access, campaign creation,
targeting, budget changes, upload, launch, or spend.

## Review before return

Audit in this order:

1. **Offer truth:** identity, terms, availability, price, state, action, and destination.
2. **Audience relevance:** recognizable intent, need, desire, objection, and vocabulary.
3. **Attraction:** a specific supported value and reason to believe, not generic hype.
4. **Voice:** recognizable brand or author under compression, without copied phrases.
5. **Message match:** query or placement, ad promise, CTA, and destination agree.
6. **Asset system:** fields fit current limits; combinations work; variants test real differences.
7. **Claims and disclosure:** substantiation, comparisons, endorsements, qualifications, policy,
   legal or regulated review, and accessibility requirements remain visible.
8. **Boundary:** return copy and notes as text; do not operate or publish the campaign.
