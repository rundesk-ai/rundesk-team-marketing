# Shape social copy and voice

## Find the message

Convert the brief into one sentence:

```text
For <audience in this encounter>, this content makes <promoted thing or idea> relevant through
<supported need, desire, tension, identity, value, or proof>, then offers <approved response>.
```

A hook is the first meaningful point of contact, not a formula. It can be a concrete result, useful
question, tension, observation, instruction, or scene established by the source. Do not manufacture
stakes, withhold the subject artificially, or use empty commands such as `stop scrolling` to cover
a weak idea. Put enough context early that the audience can decide whether the post is for them.

Build attraction in this order when the evidence supports it:

1. **Recognition:** name a situation, desire, question, or tension the audience recognizes.
2. **Relevance:** connect that moment to the actual product, offer, story, or idea being promoted.
3. **Credibility:** supply a concrete feature, example, demonstration, fact, or approved proof.
4. **Response:** offer a proportionate next step that the content and destination can fulfill.

Do not print all four as a formula. A strong visual may carry recognition; an established follower
may need less context; a story may delay the product while preserving its promise. The sequence is
an audit of why someone would care, not a caption template.

Use concrete nouns and verbs. Cut throat-clearing, vague inspiration, duplicate setup, inflated
adjectives, and unearned urgency. Short is not automatically clear, and long is not automatically
substantial. Give the idea the space its surface and audience need, within verified constraints.

## Preserve voice without performing it

Model the account or named author through observable choices: point of view, stance toward the
audience, vocabulary, syntax, rhythm, humor, metaphor, emotional range, and formality. Adapt tone to
the content moment. Do not paste memorable lines from voice samples, exaggerate quirks, invent a
person's belief or experience, or convert a brand voice into generic social-media enthusiasm.

Keep platform convention subordinate to recognizable voice. A playful surface does not require
slang; a professional audience does not require lifeless corporate prose. Emojis, fragments,
lowercase styling, and punctuation patterns are deliberate voice choices only when the evidence or
brief supports them.

Voice attracts the right audience by making the source recognizable and the relationship clear.
Keep the approved stance, vocabulary, rhythm, emotional temperature, and degree of directness while
bringing the audience's real language and stakes forward. Do not make every brand loud, witty,
urgent, intimate, or slang-heavy in the name of engagement.

## Make the parts cooperate

- **On-creative text** provides the essential orientation, sequence, or takeaway the visual needs.
- **Caption or description** adds meaning, context, evidence, voice, or the next useful step.
- **Title** names the idea accurately where the surface gives it a separate discovery role.
- **Call to action** follows from the content and available destination; it need not be a command.
- **Hashtags and keywords** describe genuine subject matter for people and discovery systems; they
  are not a substitute for useful prose or a fixed-volume growth ritual.

For organic discovery, use the audience's supplied language and the destination's actual subject.
Map one primary topic and a small set of natural supporting terms to the title, opening, caption,
description, or on-creative text where they clarify meaning. Never repeat a term to reach a density,
borrow a trending phrase unrelated to the content, or claim search demand that was not researched.
Keyword research, volume, competition, and opportunity selection remain evidence inputs; this
workflow turns approved inputs into readable copy.

Do not repeat identical language in every field unless accessibility or comprehension requires it.
Do not make a caption depend on a visual detail that was not supplied. If creative is still being
made, return a content requirement (`show the completed settings panel with the changed state`) not
fictional alt text (`a user happily clicks the new toggle`).

## Adapt rather than cross-post

For each platform or surface, reconsider:

1. who encounters it and with what intent;
2. what the content object reveals before any caption is opened;
3. which message belongs on the creative versus in supporting copy;
4. how the surface sequences information;
5. whether discovery is follower-, recommendation-, search-, or save-oriented; and
6. what honest next action is available there.

Maintain one claim ledger and voice card across variants. Change information order, framing,
length, and field use only where the surface changes the reader's job. Variants should test a real
choice—such as practical versus reflective framing—not swap synonyms around the same sentence.
That difference must exist in the source. When the evidence supports only one angle, say so and
offer honest wording treatments rather than inventing three content concepts.

The source's nouns and verbs set the outer promise. A summary that establishes only `a team changed
its status routine and an author was interviewed` does not establish why the team changed, what
happened afterward, lessons, benefits, a method, or advice. Do not smuggle any of those into a title,
question, creative overlay, or invitation to `discover` them. Likewise, a prototype result does not
mean the owner plans to continue, fix, launch, or report on it.
A behavior such as `groups saved filters into named views` likewise does not establish why it was
built, what problem it solves, that it makes filters easier to identify, or any other intended
benefit. Treat purpose supplied by an authorized owner separately from a benefit demonstrated by
evidence. Do not fill a short caption with generic domain background.

## Use ethical and accessible persuasion

Never invent scarcity, consensus, controversy, fear, identity pressure, or a customer result. Avoid
engagement bait that asks for empty interaction, misleading curiosity gaps, unrelated hashtags or
keywords, and copy that promises something the destination does not deliver. Clearly disclose a
material relationship or promotion as required; do not hide it after decorative content.

Write hashtags in readable capitalization when they contain multiple words. Do not use emoji or
visual styling as the only carrier of meaning, overload assistive-technology output with decorative
characters, or provide alt text that merely repeats the caption. Request captions or transcripts
for meaningful speech and audio, and describe the information or purpose of supplied visuals.
