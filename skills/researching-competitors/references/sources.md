# Competitor research source basis

This package is a Rundesk synthesis of securities disclosure requirements, company-registry
documentation, advertising law and self-regulatory precedent, review-platform published policy, and
peer-reviewed work on evaluation method. Use this file to audit or update any claim in the other
references.

**Read in this order of authority.** A regulation or accounting standard states a requirement. An
audited filing states a fact under obligation. A self-regulatory decision applies a standard to facts.
Peer-reviewed work measures a method's failure rate. A platform's own policy states its practice. A
press release states an intention. Never present a later tier as an earlier one.

Verified against the sources listed here in **August 2026**. Two things here move fast and must be
re-checked before being quoted: review-platform ownership and policy, and company-registry filing
requirements.

## Tier 1 — securities disclosure and accounting

- [EDGAR full-text search](https://www.sec.gov/edgar/search/) and its
  [FAQ](https://www.sec.gov/edgar/search/efts-faq.html): coverage of electronic filings **since
  2001**, no natural-language search, and no wildcards inside exact phrases or boolean searches.
- [Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/files/form10-k.pdf) and
  [Form S-1](https://www.sec.gov/files/forms-1.pdf): the item-to-regulation mapping, and the items
  unique to a registration statement — use of proceeds, determination of offering price, dilution,
  selling security holders, plan of distribution.
- Regulation S-K [Item 101 Business](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/chapter-II/part-229/section-229.101),
  [Item 105 Risk Factors](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/chapter-II/part-229/section-229.105),
  [Item 303 MD&A](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/chapter-II/part-229/section-229.303): the
  revenue-activity and competitive-conditions hooks, the discouragement of generic risk factors, and
  the requirement to attribute material revenue change to **price versus volume**.
- [Form D notice guidance](https://www.sec.gov/resources-small-businesses/exempt-offerings/filing-form-d-notice),
  [17 CFR 230.503](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/chapter-II/part-230/section-230.503), and
  [the form itself](https://www.sec.gov/files/formd.pdf): filing within 15 days of first sale; total
  offering amount, amount sold, amount remaining; optional revenue range; and **no valuation item**.
- **ASC 606-10-32-32**, that a stated or list price "may be (but shall not be presumed to be) the
  standalone selling price". The codification is gated; the language is quoted here from
  [a filing that reproduces it verbatim](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1095146/000168316822003632/athena_s1a2.htm).
  **This is a filing quoting the standard, not the standard.**

**Worked filing examples**, cited because they show what these items actually yield:
[Rapid7 FY2022 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1560327/000156032723000016/rp-20221231.htm)
— competitors named per segment, the filer's stated basis of competition, the risk-factor description
of competitors bundling at lower prices, standalone selling price estimated from historical discounts
off list, and the auditor's critical-audit-matter treatment of that estimate.
[Gartner FY2025 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/749251/000074925126000112/it-20251231.htm)
— the subsequent-events note confirming and pricing the digital-markets divestiture.

## Tier 1 — registries and intellectual property

- [UK Companies House search](https://find-and-update.company-information.service.gov.uk/),
  [API](https://developer.company-information.service.gov.uk/),
  [bulk data](https://download.companieshouse.gov.uk/en_output.html): that the registry **does not
  check the accuracy of the information filed**.
- [Accounts filing requirements](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/life-of-a-company-annual-requirements/life-of-a-company-part-1-accounts)
  and the [reform date change](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/companies-house-to-bring-in-changes-to-accounts-filing-from-april-2028):
  small companies currently need not deliver a profit and loss account; from **April 2028** they must,
  and abridged accounts end. **The date moved from April 2027 — guidance citing 2027 is stale.**
- [Confirmation statement guidance](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/filing-your-companys-confirmation-statement):
  every company, including dormant ones, must file at least yearly.
- [SBA on registering a business](https://www.sba.gov/business-guide/launch-your-business/register-your-business):
  registration is with a state office. Used here as positive evidence that there is no federal
  registry; **no source asserts that negative directly.**
- [Delaware entity search](https://icis.corp.delaware.gov/ecorp/entitysearch/namesearch.aspx) and
  [FAQs](https://corp.delaware.gov/faqs/): the seven free fields, the prohibition on data mining, and
  that shareholder information is maintained by the corporation rather than filed.
- [California business entity FAQs](https://www.sos.ca.gov/business-programs/business-entities/faqs):
  the contrasting state that publishes officers, directors, managers, members, general partners, and
  agent for service of process.
- [FinCEN beneficial ownership](https://www.fincen.gov/boi) and
  [FAQs](https://www.fincen.gov/boi-faqs): US companies are exempt and no longer required to file, the
  reporting-company definition now covers only foreign-formed entities, and the data was never public.
- USPTO: [trademark search](https://tmsearch.uspto.gov/) with its
  [documented limits](https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/search/federal-trademark-searching) — no sure
  way to find all conflicts, live marks only; [TSDR](https://tsdr.uspto.gov/);
  [Patent Public Search](https://www.uspto.gov/patents/search/patent-public-search), the October 2022
  retirement of the legacy platforms, and pre-grant publications from March 2001;
  [assignment search](https://assignmentcenter.uspto.gov/search/patent), recorded **patent**
  assignments from 1980 to the present.
- [35 U.S.C. 122](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/35/122): applications kept in confidence,
  publication promptly after **18 months** from the earliest filing date for which benefit is sought,
  and the non-publication request — which is why absence from the published database is not evidence
  that no application exists.

## Tier 1 — advertising law and self-regulatory precedent

- [16 CFR 14.15, comparative advertising policy statement](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/part-14/section-14.15)
  (also as [codified text](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CFR-2023-title16-vol1/pdf/CFR-2023-title16-vol1-sec14-15.pdf)):
  that policy encourages naming competitors while requiring clarity and disclosure; that truthful
  nondeceptive comparative advertising informs purchase decisions; that disparaging advertising is
  permissible when truthful and not deceptive; the definition requiring **objectively measurable
  attributes or price** and identification of the brand; and that a **higher** substantiation standard
  for comparative claims is inappropriate.
- [FTC policy statement on advertising substantiation](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/ftc-policy-statement-regarding-advertising-substantiation):
  a reasonable basis before dissemination, and at least the advertised level of support where a claim
  references one.
- [FTC advertising FAQs](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/advertising-faqs-guide-small-business):
  that letters from satisfied customers are not sufficient to substantiate a claim requiring objective
  evaluation.
- [16 CFR Part 465, consumer reviews and testimonials](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/part-465)
  — [89 FR 68034, 22 August 2024](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2024-08-22/pdf/2024-18519.pdf),
  effective **21 October 2024**: the prohibitions, and the generalized-solicitation carve-out.
- [16 CFR Part 255, endorsement guides](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/part-255):
  that an advertiser must substantiate endorsement claims as if made directly, and that **consumer
  endorsements are not competent and reliable scientific evidence**.
- [FTC guidance on the reviews rule](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-reviews-testimonials-rule-questions-answers)
  and [on soliciting and paying for reviews](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/soliciting-paying-online-reviews-guide-marketers):
  that incentives are not prohibited absent a sentiment requirement, that non-disclosure of incentives
  may still violate the statute, and that a paid platform relationship should be disclosed.
- Enforcement, each from the Commission's own release:
  [review suppression below four stars](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2022/01/fashion-nova-will-pay-42-million-part-settlement-ftc-allegations-it-blocked-negative-reviews),
  [managers reviewing their own products via fake accounts and sale of fake influence indicators](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2019/10/devumi-owner-ceo-settle-ftc-charges-they-sold-fake-indicators-social-media-influence-cosmetics-firm),
  [tens of thousands of fake reviews diluting real ones](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2022/08/ftc-states-sue-rental-listing-platform-roomster-its-owners-duping-prospective-renters-fake-reviews),
  and [supplying the means to generate deceptive review content](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/09/ftc-announces-crackdown-deceptive-ai-claims-schemes).
- National Advertising Division: the ["#1" claims discussion](https://bbbprograms.org/media/insights/podcast/number-one-claims)
  establishing that such claims are not puffery and that how many hold an opinion is provable; the
  [case digest](https://bbbnp-bbbp-stf-use1-01.s3.amazonaws.com/docs/default-source/bbb-national-programs/case-digests/naddigest_2022_digital_5.20.22.pdf)
  carrying the **85% of the relevant marketplace** general rule, the finding that a review platform's
  data did not provide reliable evidence for a "Rated #1" claim, and that star ratings do not indicate
  why a rating was given; and the [software-assistant decision](https://bbbprograms.org/media/newsroom/decisions/microsoft-copilot)
  on implied capability breadth.

## Tier 2 — peer-reviewed method

| Source | Establishes |
|---|---|
| van der Kouwe, Andriesse, Bos, Giuffrida & Heiser, *Benchmarking Crimes: An Emerging Threat in Systems Security*, [arXiv 1801.02381](https://arxiv.org/abs/1801.02381) | 22 named benchmarking crimes; a survey of 50 top-venue papers finding an average of five per tier-1 paper and only one paper committing none |
| [Heiser's benchmarking-crimes taxonomy](https://gernot-heiser.org/benchmarking-crimes.html) | Selective benchmarking as "the mother of all benchmarking crimes"; the ethics of benchmarking a competitor's system; stale baselines; complete results over ratios. **Self-published by a named domain expert, not peer-reviewed** |
| García-Fernández et al., [arXiv 2409.15150](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.15150) | SaaS pricing configuration space grows exponentially with add-ons; frequency of pricing change is increasing |
| García-Fernández et al., [arXiv 2503.21444](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.21444) | Errors identified in 35 of more than 150 modelled pricings — published pricing pages that do not internally cohere |
| Acemoglu, Autor, Hazell & Restrepo, *AI and Jobs: Evidence from Online Vacancies*, [NBER WP 28257](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w28257/w28257.pdf) | That adoption is **partially** identified from hiring footprints; the source-concentration cap in vacancy data; and that online postings overrepresent technical and professional roles |
| [RFC 6962, Certificate Transparency](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc6962.txt) and [certificate.transparency.dev](https://certificate.transparency.dev/howctworks/) | Publicly auditable append-only logs of issued certificates, and that **anyone can submit a chain** |

Artifact evidence tiers are adapted from ACM's artifact review and badging vocabulary — functional,
available, results reproduced, results replicated. The primary page blocks automated retrieval; the
definitions were read from [SIGIR's verbatim reproduction](https://sigir.org/general-information/acm-sigir-artifact-badging/),
which is noted here rather than presented as the primary source.

## Tier 2 — platform policy, and how it was read

Every major review platform blocks automated retrieval of its main site. Policy quoted in
`review-platforms.md` came from live fetches where the host allowed it —
[G2 community guidelines](https://legal.g2.com/community-guidelines),
[G2 advertising](https://sell.g2.com/quick-start-guides/drive-growth/learn-about-g2-ads),
[Trustpilot business guidelines](https://corporate.trustpilot.com/legal/for-businesses/guidelines-for-businesses/jun-2026),
[Trustpilot trust report](https://corporate.trustpilot.com/trust/trust-report-2025) — and from
archived snapshots of the publisher's own page where it did not, including the Capterra and GetApp
shared guidelines, the pay-per-click service description, TrustRadius's review-provenance and scoring
pages, and the former owner's digital-markets landing page. **A snapshot is the publisher's text at a
date, not the live page. Re-check before quoting to a client.**

Ownership of the acquired platforms is corroborated four ways: the
[acquisition announcement](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/g2-to-acquire-capterra-software-advice-and-getapp-from-gartner-302673901.html),
the seller's 10-K subsequent-events note above, the former owner's own landing-page notice, and the
shared guidelines' defined operator.

## What this package deliberately does not cite

- **ISO/IEC 25010** and the **DESMET** evaluation methodology. Both are the obvious standards for
  defining comparison criteria and both are gated. Neither was opened, so neither is cited or
  paraphrased here.
- **IFRS 15 paragraph 77**, the international counterpart of the list-price provision, which could not
  be retrieved. Only the US codification language is used, and only as quoted inside a filing.
- **Porter's articles** beyond their editorial abstracts, which is all that was readable. Competitive
  strategy is named as the academically grounded strand without attributing specific arguments.
- **Positioning practitioner works** as evidence. They are the origin of widely used vocabulary and are
  cited as convention, by named author, with no empirical validation claimed.
- **Any statement that a review platform's paid placement does not affect its scores or rankings.** One
  platform claims it explicitly; the largest group does not address it anywhere. The honest report is
  that the relationship is undocumented, not that it is benign or that it is corrupt.
- **A pay-per-lead review product**, which could not be verified as existing beyond a forward-looking
  mention.
- **Any technology-detection accuracy figure**, since only one vendor publishes an accuracy statement
  and it is a disclaimer rather than a measurement.
- **A "2025 fake review count."** The published figure is for data year 2024.
- **An LLM-summarized page as a citation.** During this package's research a summarizing fetch returned
  a fabricated pseudo-quote from a real page. Every quotation here was confirmed against retrieved
  document text.
