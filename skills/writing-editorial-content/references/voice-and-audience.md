# Model voice for a real audience

Voice matching begins with authority and evidence. A fluent imitation built from guesses is not a
voice model; it is a new character presented under somebody else's name.

## Separate four controls

| Control | What it governs | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Product or house voice | The organization's stable relationship with readers | Approved style guide, lexicon, published product copy, brand principles |
| Author voice | The named writer's recurring point of view and language behavior | Approved first-person samples, interviews, notes, edits, stated preferences |
| Tone | How the stable voice adapts to this subject, audience state, and stakes | The brief, reader situation, channel, sensitivity, urgency |
| Format convention | What readers expect from this kind of piece and publication | Publication examples, template, editor direction, channel constraints |

When these conflict, do not silently blend them. Preserve mandatory house, legal, terminology, and
format constraints; surface a material conflict between the author and product voice for editorial
decision.

## Build an evidence-based voice card

Analyze representative approved samples across more than one topic. A single launch announcement
may show the tone of that moment rather than the author's stable voice.

```text
Point of view and stance:
Relationship to the reader:
Knowledge assumed and explained:
Vocabulary and recurring domain terms:
Sentence length, shape, and variation:
Paragraph rhythm and transitions:
Concrete detail, analogy, and metaphor:
Humor, warmth, intensity, and restraint:
How uncertainty, disagreement, and failure are handled:
Openings and endings:
Patterns to preserve:
Patterns to avoid or use sparingly:
Evidence coverage and confidence:
```

Describe behavior rather than copying phrases. `Uses short declarative sentences after technical
explanations to state the consequence` is usable. `Sounds punchy` is not. Keep rare stylistic tics
rare; increasing their frequency creates parody.

Samples authorize analysis, not republication. `Approved sample`, `preserve this voice`, and `match
this voice` do not approve the sample's words for the new piece. Unless the owner separately says to
quote or use the exact line, do not paste it into the draft, turn it into a quotation, or use its most
distinctive phrase as a slogan. Write new language from the observed patterns.

When no approved samples or stated voice exists, use a neutral clear draft and return the missing
voice choice. Do not invent a backstory, beliefs, humor, favorite metaphors, or personal experience
for the named author or character.

## Model the audience as a reading situation

Demographics alone rarely decide the prose. Record:

- why these readers encounter the piece and what competes for their attention;
- what they already know, believe, fear, want, or disagree about;
- the task, decision, understanding, feeling, or conversation the piece should enable;
- the domain language they use and terms that need explanation;
- the evidence they will trust and objections the piece must answer;
- their likely reading mode: scanning, close reading, reference, discovery, or entertainment;
- accessibility, language, cultural, device, and translation needs; and
- what would waste their time, patronize them, exclude them, or break trust.

Write to the most specific supported audience. `Developers` is incomplete when the difference
between a maintainer evaluating an architecture choice and a beginner following a release story
changes the content. Do not invent a persona to make an unknown audience appear researched.

## Adapt tone without abandoning voice

Keep the stable voice while changing intensity, explanation, humor, formality, and pace for the
reader's situation. A failure postmortem, celebratory launch, reflective column, and beginner article
can sound like the same author without sounding emotionally identical.

Test the result:

- Could the intended reader recognize that this is for them before the midpoint?
- Does the piece use knowledge the author or product can credibly possess?
- Are voice choices observable in the prose without relying on catchphrases?
- Does tone respect the subject and reader state?
- Would removing the byline leave a distinctive point of view, or only generic polish?
- Has clarity been sacrificed to perform personality?
