# Records a company was obliged to file

## Full-text filing search, and its hard boundary

Electronic filing full-text search covers filings **submitted since 2001** and no earlier. Natural
language search is not supported, and wildcards do not work inside exact phrases or boolean searches.
A question about a company's pre-2001 history needs a different route entirely.

## What each annual-report item actually requires

The annual report's business and risk sections point to disclosure items, and knowing which item
governs tells you what you are entitled to expect.

- **Business** — after its move to a principles-based form there is no longer a mandatory
  "Competition" heading. The hooks that still produce competitive detail are the requirement to
  describe revenue-generating activities, products and services, and any dependence on key products,
  services, or customers including governmental ones; the status of development efforts for new or
  enhanced products together with **trends in market demand and competitive conditions**; and any
  material change to a previously disclosed business strategy.
- **Risk factors** — a discussion of the material factors making an investment speculative or risky,
  with generic risks explicitly discouraged. This is where competitive pressure gets described
  candidly, because understating it creates liability.
- **Management's discussion and analysis** — the pricing hook. Where revenue changed materially
  period over period, the filer must describe the extent to which the change is attributable to
  **changes in prices** versus changes in volume, or to new products. That is a rival telling you
  whether it grew by raising prices or by selling more.

**What this yields in practice.** Annual reports routinely name competitors segment by segment and
state the filer's own basis of competition — functionality, breadth, total cost of ownership. Risk
sections then describe the mechanism, including rivals bundling at a lower price as part of a larger
sale and driving average selling prices down. That is competitive intelligence written by the
competitor, under penalty.

**Subsequent-events notes are underused.** A press release announces a transaction; the note in the
next filing confirms it closed and prices it. When a deal matters to your analysis, find the note.

## What a registration statement adds

For a company going public, the registration statement is the first appearance of audited financials
and a full business and MD&A narrative. It also carries items an annual report never does: use of
proceeds, **determination of offering price**, dilution, selling security holders, and plan of
distribution. Pre-revenue filers must additionally describe their plan of operation.

If a rival has filed to go public, that document is the most detailed account of their business that
will ever exist publicly.

## Private companies

**United Kingdom.** A central register with free search and a free API, plus bulk downloads. Two
things to hold onto:

- The registry **does not check the accuracy of the information filed**. It is a repository of
  filings, not a verified dataset.
- Small companies currently need not deliver a profit and loss account or directors' report. That
  changes: from **April 2028** delivering a profit and loss account becomes mandatory and abridged
  accounts are abolished. Guidance citing April 2027 is out of date — the date moved.

The annual confirmation statement keeps directors, people with significant control, and the
registered office current, and every company including dormant ones must file one at least yearly.

**United States.** There is no federal company registry; registration is at state level, and what is
public varies enormously by state.

- One major incorporation state publishes only entity name, file number, formation date, registered
  agent, address, phone and residency for free, and prohibits data mining. Shareholders are not on
  file with the state at all — the corporation keeps that itself. **Directors are filed annually but
  sit outside the free fields**, in a paid annual-report copy; limited-liability company members and
  managers need not be listed at all. So "that state does not disclose officers and directors" is too
  blunt to publish — it is a question of which record and at what cost.
- Other states publish officers, directors, managers, members, general partners, and the agent for
  service of process in a free search. When a state matters to your analysis, check what that
  specific state publishes rather than generalizing.

**Beneficial ownership is now a dead end for US companies.** The reporting requirement was revised so
that only entities formed under the law of a foreign country are reporting companies; US companies are
exempt and no longer required to file. The data was never public in any case — it sits in a
non-public database.

## Intellectual property

**Trademarks.** The current search system replaced the legacy one. Read its own caveats: there is no
sure way to uncover all potentially conflicting marks, and the official search considers only live
marks. Status and prosecution documents live in a separate document-retrieval system.

**Patents.** The legacy platforms were retired in October 2022 in favour of a single public search;
pre-grant publications run from March 2001 forward. Assignment records — which is how you see
ownership move — cover recorded patent assignments from 1980 to the present. No comparable coverage
range for trademark assignments was established, so do not assert one.

**The lag that defeats naive patent scanning.** Applications are kept in confidence, and each is
published promptly after **18 months** from the earliest filing date for which benefit is sought. A
filer may also request non-publication where no foreign counterpart is filed. Therefore **absence
from the published-application database is not evidence that no application exists.** Anyone reading
patent activity as a roadmap is reading, at best, an 18-month-old and incomplete signal.

## Funding

A securities exemption notice must be filed within **15 days after the first sale** in the offering.
It states the total offering amount, the total amount sold, and the amount remaining, with an optional
revenue range the issuer may decline to disclose.

**There is no valuation item on the form.** A press-release valuation is not supported by the filing,
and citing the filing as if it were is a misattribution. Report the amount sold from the notice, and a
valuation only as the claim of whoever announced it.

## The reading order

Filings, then registry, then their own publications, then the press. Work down only as far as you
need, and when a lower tier contradicts a higher one, the higher one wins and the contradiction is
itself worth reporting.
