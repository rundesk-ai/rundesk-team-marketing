# Social-writing source map

Use this file to audit a lesson, not as a second procedure. Platform features and recommendations
change; verify current mechanics when they matter. Verified 2 September 2026 against the linked
pages.

## Platform forms and discovery

- [Meta: Introducing Best Practices, an Education Hub for Creators on Instagram](https://about.fb.com/news/2024/10/best-practices-education-hub-creators-instagram/)
  says Instagram's professional dashboard supplies general and personalized guidance across
  creation, engagement, reach, monetization, and guidelines, including evolving advice about
  attention, Reels, audio, and hashtags. This supports verifying account-specific optimization
  rather than encoding universal formulas; it does not publish a complete caption style system.
- [Meta: New Ways to Create Content on Instagram](https://about.fb.com/news/2023/11/new-ways-to-create-content-on-instagram/)
  distinguishes Reels, feed photos, carousels, and Stories as separate creation surfaces. It
  supports routing copy by surface but does not establish that every post needs every field.

## Pinterest mechanics and first-party recommendations

- [Pinterest Business: Audience](https://business.pinterest.com/audience/)
  reports 640 million monthly active users in Q2 2026 and says 96% of the top 1,000 global searches
  in its June 2023 analysis were unbranded. Those are dated, platform-reported marketing figures,
  not proof that a named buyer segment is reachable or commercially valuable.
- [Pinterest Business Help: Pin performance and distribution](https://help.pinterest.com/en/business/article/pin-performance-and-distribution)
  says search, saves, and engagement inform distribution; Pins have no set engagement window; and
  language, location, season, format, and trends affect results. It recommends original content at
  least weekly, 2:3 high-quality creative, relevant titles and descriptions, link and landing-page
  alignment, fresh rather than duplicate Pins, and account-level performance review. These are
  current platform recommendations, not a guaranteed ranking formula.
- [Pinterest Business Help: Pinterest Analytics](https://help.pinterest.com/en/business/article/pinterest-analytics)
  defines impressions, saves, save rate, engagements, engagement rate, Pin clicks, outbound clicks,
  outbound-click rate, video measures, and audience measures. It supports keeping platform actions
  separate; it does not establish downstream conversion, realized value, or causality.
- [Pinterest Business Help: Pinterest Trends](https://help.pinterest.com/en/business/article/pinterest-trends)
  exposes search, save, shopping, audience, and seasonal trend curves, including when planning for a
  Moment begins and how interest changes. It supports timing from market-specific demand rather than
  a generic social-posting calendar.
- [Pinterest Business: 2026 Marketing Moments Guide](https://business.pinterest.com/en-us/blog/pinterest-brand-moments-guide-2026/)
  says seasonal searches can build months before the event and gives an illustrative April launch
  for a June peak. This supports starting at observed lift-off; it is advertiser guidance and does
  not create one universal two-month lead time.
- [Pinterest Help: Create a Pin from an image or video](https://help.pinterest.com/en/article/create-a-pin-from-an-image-or-video)
  documents current organic creation fields and the format distinction: selecting up to ten images
  on desktop creates separate Pins, while selecting multiple assets in the mobile flow stitches them
  into one video Pin. It also shows title, description, link, board, related-topic, product-tag,
  accessibility, and scheduling controls. The skill treats those as production requirements and
  grants no account mutation.
- [Pinterest Business Help: Create a carousel](https://help.pinterest.com/en/business/article/create-a-carousel)
  defines a swipeable multi-card Pin and currently limits its creation to business accounts in Ads
  Manager or the bulk editor. It supports routing carousel copy to paid advertising instead of
  promising an organic gallery feature.
- [Pinterest Help: Review Pin specs](https://help.pinterest.com/en/article/review-pin-specs)
  is the current source for supported file types, sizes, aspect ratios, and field limits. The skill
  uses a 2:3 standard-Pin default but requires format-specific verification rather than freezing the
  complete spec table.
- [Pinterest Business: Creative best practices](https://business.pinterest.com/creative-best-practices/)
  recommends vertical mobile creative, visible but subordinate branding, products shown clearly and
  in realistic settings, concise messaging, and clear visual hierarchy for ads. The visual lessons
  are useful experiment candidates for organic commercial Pins, but the page does not prove the
  same performance effect for every organic account or category.
- [Pinterest Business Help: Add alternative text](https://help.pinterest.com/en/business/article/add-alternative-text-to-your-pins)
  says alt text explains the visual for people using screen readers. This supports writing a useful
  visual alternative rather than hiding discovery keywords in it.

## Pinterest shopping and monetization mechanics

- [Pinterest Help: Become a Pinterest creator](https://help.pinterest.com/en/resources/creators)
  identifies current creator paths through affiliate-linked products and brand partnerships and
  points to business-account analytics. It describes possibilities, not typical income.
- [Pinterest Business Help: Shopping on Pinterest](https://help.pinterest.com/en/business/guide/shopping-on-pinterest)
  distinguishes catalog-created Product Pins from product rich Pins, says both distribute
  organically, and says only catalog-created Product Pins can be promoted as shopping ads. It also
  requires current product information.
- [Pinterest Business Help: Retail catalogs](https://help.pinterest.com/en/business/article/before-you-get-started-with-catalogs)
  requires a business account, claimed site, Pinterest tag, and merchant-compliant site; documents
  daily catalog access and optional metadata; and says additional product-image links create new
  Pins. These are implementation and data-quality requirements outside the writing authority.
- [Pinterest Business Help: Collections Pins](https://help.pinterest.com/en/business/article/shop-the-look-product-tagging)
  documents a hero image or video with three or more tagged products. It supports a coherent
  shoppable scene, not a claim that collections always beat one-product Pins.
- [Pinterest Business Help: Paid partnerships](https://help.pinterest.com/en/business/article/paid-partnerships-for-creators)
  says the creator and brand establish the relationship outside Pinterest, the creator applies the
  label before publication, and the brand approves its name. The skill records those as handoff
  requirements and does not negotiate or publish.
- [Pinterest Commercial and Branded Content Guidelines](https://policy.pinterest.com/en/commercial-and-branded-content-guidelines)
  requires affiliate content to be original, add value, disclose its commercial nature, use an
  authentic presence, and avoid fake accounts or traffic manipulation. These platform rules apply
  even when a Pin is organic.
- [FTC: Endorsement Guides—What People Are Asking](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftcs-endorsement-guides-what-people-are-asking)
  says material connections should be clear and conspicuous and that a platform disclosure tool may
  not be sufficient on its own. It supports a nearby, understandable disclosure; the package does
  not provide legal approval or replace jurisdiction-specific review.

## Observational research and selected success reports

- Tailwind's [2025 benchmark methodology and evergreen results](https://www.tailwindapp.com/pinterest-marketing/research/2025-benchmark-study-part-1),
  [creative findings](https://www.tailwindapp.com/pinterest-marketing/research/2025-benchmark-study-part-2),
  and [fresh-Pin analysis](https://www.tailwindapp.com/pinterest-marketing/research/2025-benchmark-study-part-three)
  cover about 1.2 million Pins across more than 17,000 accounts, with 2023–2024 observation windows.
  The study reports long-lived engagement, image-format prevalence, category-dependent raw versus
  designed imagery, focused titles and descriptions, declining distribution from repeated
  image-and-URL combinations, and no universal best posting hour. It is observational research from
  a Pinterest scheduling vendor whose customers are selected; correlations generate tests and do
  not prove that a tactic or Tailwind caused performance.
- Pinterest's [Adrian Widjy creator case study](https://create.pinterest.com/en-gb/success-stories/adrian-widjy/)
  reports that affiliate-linked, visually compelling, informative Pins and later brand promotion of
  eight already-converting Pins increased one creator's affiliate earnings and clicks. It is a
  selected platform case with paid amplification, one creator, and no control; it supports testing
  authentic product-in-use content and promoting proven work, not forecasting a multiple.
- Pinterest's [Pink Seed Marketing creator story](https://create.pinterest.com/en-ca/success-stories/pink-seed-marketing/)
  describes varying destinations and content areas, then reviewing analytics across channels. It
  supports portfolio balance and destination-level learning but provides no controlled evidence for
  a universal mix.
- Elaine Timms' [food-creator case study](https://elainetimms.com/evergreen-growth-how-food-creator-pins-for-profit/)
  self-reports increased outbound clicks, saves, traffic, and revenue after intent-led keywords,
  fresh creative variants, design tests, consistent production, and destination monetization. Her
  [gardening case study](https://elainetimms.com/pinterest-management-case-study-traffic-revenue-blog/)
  reports a two-year archive built from intent-specific overlays, broader keyword coverage, and
  seasonal lead time. Both are service-provider marketing case studies without independent
  verification or controls; their repeated sequence informs tests, not expected results.

## Writing, claims, and accessibility

- [Mailchimp Content Style Guide: Voice and tone](https://styleguide.mailchimp.com/voice-and-tone/)
  distinguishes a relatively stable voice from tone adapted to a reader's state. Its specific house
  personality is not copied; the distinction supports consistent cross-platform voice.
- [Google Ads Help: Write compelling, genuine ad copy](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6167101?hl=en-AU)
  advises beginning with user needs and benefits and showing value without becoming pushy. This is
  paid-search guidance, but it supports the durable audience-relevance distinction used for organic
  promotional copy; it does not make every social post an ad or supply a universal hook.
- [Federal Trade Commission: Advertising and Marketing](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/advertising-marketing)
  states that advertising claims must be truthful, non-deceptive, and evidence-based, with
  additional rules for some subjects. The package preserves substantiation and disclosure gates but
  does not provide legal approval.
- [W3C Web Accessibility Initiative: Writing for web accessibility](https://www.w3.org/WAI/tips/writing/)
  supports informative text, meaningful alternatives, clear instructions, and keeping meaning
  available beyond decoration. Its web guidance informs social copy but does not document each
  platform's accessibility implementation.

## Catalog conclusions and limits

- `Instagram feed`, `carousel`, `Reel`, `Story`, and `Pinterest Pin` are separated because their
  content objects, sequences, fields, and audience moments differ. The package's return schema is a
  local production control, not a platform requirement.
- The no-posting boundary follows the distinction between content authorship and an external account
  mutation. Platform creation pages describe publication actions; they do not imply that a writing
  request authorizes them.
- No linked source proves a universal caption length, opening formula, hashtag or keyword count,
  emoji style, call to action, product/editorial ratio, daily volume, posting hour, cross-posting
  method, time-to-results, or performance outcome. Current platform guidance, Pinterest Trends, and
  first-party account and destination evidence override generic advice.
- The recognition, relevance, credibility, and response audit combines the cited audience, voice,
  truth, destination, and platform lessons. Its four labels are a local copy-review control, not a
  published platform formula or guaranteed attraction sequence.
- The Pinterest traffic path, content lanes, format table, monetization bridge, and return schema are
  this catalog's operational synthesis. The sources establish mechanics and candidate patterns;
  they do not establish that Pinterest is the right investment or that any content plan will earn
  traffic, followers, or revenue.
