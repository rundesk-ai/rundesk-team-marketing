# Turn evidence into a usable answer

A cited result should let a reader understand the answer first and audit it second. Do not make the
reader reconstruct the conclusion from a bibliography.

## Build from the ledger

Draft from verified evidence notes. For each sentence, know whether it is:

- a fact directly established by a source;
- a synthesis supported by several sources;
- an inference from stated evidence;
- a recommendation based on an explicit objective; or
- an uncertainty or evidence gap.

Label inference and uncertainty in the prose. A citation does not convert an inference into a fact.

## Cite at claim level

- Place citations immediately after the supported claim or short group of claims.
- Link the exact page, section, record, or persistent identifier rather than a homepage or search
  result.
- Preserve author or organization, title, publication date, and DOI or stable URL when the requested
  citation style needs them.
- Cite paraphrases as well as quotations. Use quotation marks for exact words and quote sparingly.
- Split a sentence when its citation supports only one clause.
- Cite the original work when read and understood. If only a secondary account was available, make
  that relationship explicit.
- Never cite a source that was not opened, an abstract as proof of unreported details, or a snippet
  that may be truncated.

```text
Bad:  put five citations at the end of a paragraph containing unrelated claims
Good: attach each source to the sentence it establishes; consolidate only when all sources support
      the same bounded claim

Bad:  lightly reword a passage and omit attribution
Good: restate the idea independently, preserve its meaning, and cite its source
```

## Shape the deliverable

For a narrow answer:

1. direct answer;
2. decisive evidence;
3. qualification or confidence; and
4. specific citation.

For a broad briefing:

1. bottom line;
2. findings organized by theme or decision;
3. evidence strength and important disagreement;
4. implications or recommended action;
5. limits, gaps, and next checks; and
6. claim-level citations.

For a rigorous review, add the protocol, search date, sources searched, selection flow, included
evidence, exclusions required by the reporting standard, and method limitations.

Use a table only for repeated comparisons with stable fields. Prose is better for a causal argument,
nuance, or a small number of findings.

## Audit the final answer

Read once for the decision and once against the evidence:

- Does the opening answer the actual question?
- Is every material factual claim traceable to evidence of the right scope?
- Does each citation support the wording immediately before it?
- Are fact, inference, recommendation, and uncertainty distinguishable?
- Are source conflicts and evidence limits represented proportionately?
- Were current facts, versions, corrections, and persistent links rechecked?
- Can repetition, source-by-source narration, or decorative detail be removed?

Do not claim certainty from source count. Explain confidence through evidence quality, independence,
agreement, and applicability.
